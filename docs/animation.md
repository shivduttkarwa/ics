# Animation

Motion is driven by `data-anim-*` attributes in the templates; no template or block writes GSAP by hand. The attributes are plain HTML and pass through Wagtail templates untouched.

| File | Does |
|---|---|
| `scripts/motion.js` | registers GSAP's plugins and the house eases (`soft`, `silk`, `glide`), exports the media queries (`still`, `phone`, `fine`) |
| `scripts/anim.js` | Lenis smooth scroll, reveals, split titles, counters, the parallax fallback; `window.Anim` |
| `scripts/slider.js` | `data-slider`: card rows (Swiper) |
| `scripts/stage.js` | `data-stage`: the full-width slide show |
| `scripts/marquee.js` | `data-marquee`: logo rows and the Instagram reel |
| `scripts/accordion.js`, `menu.js`, `news.js`, `header.js` | the FAQ rows, the main menu, the news photo swap, the room the header's bar keeps for its fixed pill |
| `scripts/stories.js` | `data-story`: a graduate story opening in place |
| `scripts/moments.js` | `data-moments`: the Faith page's moments, held on screen and uncovered in turn |
| `06-animation/_animation.less`, `_parallax.less` | start states, line masks, the scroll-driven parallax |

## How it feels, and why it stays smooth
- Everything moves on `transform` and `opacity` only (plus `clip-path` for photo wipes), at sub-pixel precision on its own compositor layer, then drops the layer when it lands so text is crisp at rest.
- No sub-pixel tails: a layer that is still creeping by fractions of a pixel is resampled every frame, so its text and hairlines look soft and seem to shiver while they are already solid. `landing()` in `motion.js` ends every move once it stays within one device pixel of home, after any overshoot (the curve keeps its shape, the tween runs that share of its duration); `settled(el, vars)` applies it to a tween from wherever the element is. Every reveal, title line, menu row, slide count and accordion panel goes through it.
- Every split title's lines glide on the compositor (`element.animate`, the house curve with its tail cut, as a CSS `linear()` easing), so their pace holds while the page is busy or scrolling, and they stay on their layers once landed, the split kept until the width changes: at 100% zoom text on a layer is drawn up to half a pixel from where the page draws it (measured: 0.25px across, 0.43px down), so a line leaving its layer, or the markup going back, visibly shifted the title. St Luke's holds its rows on layers the same way. Section titles used to rise on the main thread, drop their layers line by line and put the markup back after the last, which read as a shiver and a final shift (2026-11-18). First-screen copy that lifts (`fade-up-soft`) keeps `will-change: transform` in its own CSS for the same reason.

- Frosted elements (backdrop-filter) fade in place: their blur is sampled from what is behind them, so one that moves over a still backdrop jitters. `glassy(el)` turns a moving preset into `fade` for such an element unless it carries its own photo (a card whose backdrop moves with it). A fade over glass runs on the glass itself and on the plain parts beside it, never on a wrapper (`fadeParts`): opacity under 1 on an ancestor cuts a backdrop-filter off from what is behind it, so the glass showed only its tint and snapped to its blur as the fade ended.
- Reveals are timed, not scrubbed: each plays once when it comes into view, at its own pace however fast the page is scrolled. Reveals that enter together start 0.09s apart in page order (0.6s at most for a whole batch).
- Long, soft curves: `soft` (`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`, the same as `--ease-out`) for moves, `soft` again for title lines (solid, no fade), `rise` (`0.33, 1, 0.68, 1`, `--ease-rise`) for first-screen lifts, `silk` (`0.16, 1, 0.3, 1`) for wipes, `glide` (`0.65, 0, 0.35, 1`) for slide changes. Nothing bounces, with one exception: the hero's video card (`from-right-edge`) springs in (`spring()` in `motion.js`: in place at about a quarter of its length, about 8% past, then back to rest with no second swing, since that faint swing read as the card shifting after it had landed), at the client's ask.
- Parallax runs as a CSS scroll-driven animation where the browser has them (Chrome, Edge, Safari 26), on the compositor and in step with the scroll, on phones too. Elsewhere GSAP follows Lenis on desktop, and touch screens without scroll timelines get none (a script-driven parallax trails a touch scroll and shivers).
- A CSS `transition` on a revealed element is switched off while its reveal runs, so the two never fight.
- Uncovered in place (the client's brief): the footer's lower band waits stuck to the foot of the screen (`position: sticky; bottom: 0`) under the section before it, which sits above it (`z-index: 1`) and scrolls up off it. It is plain CSS, so it holds under reduced motion and on every device, and the band carries no `data-anim` of its own.
- The announcement bar is gsa.uk.com's, measured there: its sheet (`.announcement__sheet`: the dark ground, the text and the corner fill under the hero's foot, as one piece) is tucked wholly up under the foot (GSA leaves a sliver showing; the client wanted none), and slides down out from under it as the page scrolls, from 40px of scroll and over the next 120px for its full height, so at about half the scroll's pace, its corners rounding off on the way so the foot's own rounded corners stay dark while it is tucked. Scrolling back up it holds where it is until the shorter way home (50px from the 40) catches it, so it is not pulled back under the foot the moment the page turns (`data-anim="tuck"` below). It eases towards that scroll target (90% of the way in about 0.6s) rather than tracking it, so a fast flick still gives a slow glide, as GSA's own script does. The bar's own box carries the next section's ground (canopy), so nothing light shows under the sheet while it is still up. Without the script, or under reduced motion, the sheet simply sits in place.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no smooth scroll, no reveals (everything simply shows), no parallax, no stack, no autoplay; sliders and marquees change instantly or stand still.

## Reveal — `data-anim="reveal"`
| Attribute | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `data-anim-type` | `fade-up` `fade-up-soft` (first-screen copy, the announcement bar's contents and the FAQ rows, whose hairlines shimmered under the slower `fade-up`: 25 up on the compositor over 0.6s on `rise`, fading evenly with it, on a held layer; give the element `will-change: transform`; a frosted button inside fades on its own, its wrapper lifting) `fade-down` (the header's items: 24 down on the compositor over 0.7s, landing under the fade, on a held layer) `fade` `from-left` `from-right` `rise` (cards) `scale` `zoom-out` (a background settling) `line` (a rule drawing from the left) `wipe-up` `wipe-down` `wipe-left` `wipe-right` (a frame opening while the photo or video inside settles from 1.2) `from-right-edge` (springs in from beyond the right edge of the screen on the compositor, a `glide`: give the element `will-change: transform` so it settles with no change) | `fade-up` |
| `data-anim-tier` | `hero` (plays on load, in `data-anim-order`) · `secondary` (starts at `top 96%`) · default (starts at `top 88%`) | default |
| `data-anim-order` | hero tier only: the step of the entrance; steps of one order start together, and each order starts as the one before it lands, minus the lead | `0` |
| `data-anim-lead` | hero tier only: seconds before the previous order lands that this one starts; negative for a pause after it | `0.4` |
| `data-anim-landed` | hero tier only: seconds after its start when a long step reads as landed, so the next needn't wait for its tail (the hero photo's settle, 0.8) | its length |
| `data-anim-delay` | seconds added to its start | `0` |
| `data-anim-duration` | seconds, overrides the preset's length (for a glide the move takes this long and its fade keeps its share: the hero's video card, 1.3) | preset |
| `data-anim-fade` | seconds: the fade-in runs evenly over this long, past the end of the move, so text is still faint while its move slows and turns solid once it has stopped (the hero intro, 1.1) | preset |
| `data-anim-start` | ScrollTrigger start, e.g. `top 70%` (`-mobile` twin below lg); a plain number is a scroll position in px | tier |
| `data-anim-type-mobile` | another preset below lg | |
| `data-anim-media` | a media query; where it does not match at load the element simply shows | |
| `data-anim-trigger` | on an ancestor: that ancestor's position starts the reveal | the element |

```html
<p data-anim="reveal">…</p>
<figure class="pillar__frame" data-anim="reveal" data-anim-type="wipe-up">…</figure>
```

### Groups — `data-anim-group="<type>"`
The direct children become reveals of that type, in order. One static attribute on the parent, the same in Nunjucks and Django: the way to stagger a loop. Never hardcode `data-anim-order` inside a loop. `data-anim-tier`, `data-anim-start` and `data-anim-media` on the group pass to its children; with the hero tier, the children share the group's `data-anim-order` and follow each other `data-anim-stagger` seconds apart (0.07). An item not displayed at that width gives up its turn.

When the items are not the group's direct children, mark each with `data-anim-item`: the group then reveals its `[data-anim-item]` descendants in page order and leaves the wrappers between them alone. The header does this: the logo, each `<li>` of the nav loop, the pill, and the button and burger inside it are items of the bar (`{% set anim_item = true %}` before the button include adds the attribute). The frosted pill carries its own `fade` type, so it takes its turn fading in place while its contents slide down.

```html
<ul class="usps__list" data-anim-group="rise">{% for card in value.cards %}<li>…</li>{% endfor %}</ul>
```

## Split titles — `data-anim="split"`
The title is split where the browser wrapped it and each line rises out of a mask, solid (1.4s on `soft`, its tail cut, no fade: a line fading as it rises reads as a fade-up, not as coming from under the mask), 0.11s apart (`data-anim-stagger` sets another gap in seconds, `data-anim-fade` adds an even fade of that length), on the compositor, each line held on its layer once landed; the original markup goes back only if the width changes, so the lines can rewrap. `data-anim-type="words"` or `"chars"` fades the pieces up instead. Takes `data-anim-tier`, `-order`, `-delay`, `-start` as a reveal does. A hero-tier title is split during setup, before the entrance starts; any other is split as it starts to play.

```html
<h2 data-anim="split">Preparing for <em>remarkable</em> futures.</h2>
```

## Counters — `data-anim="counter"`
The first number in the element (or in its `[data-anim-count]` child) counts up from 0 over 2.2s as it comes into view. Prefixes, suffixes, thousands separators and decimals are kept (`~900`, `150+`, `100%`, `99.90`); text with no digits (`XX%`) is left alone.

## Parallax — `data-anim="parallax"`
The layer travels from `--parallax-from` to `--parallax-to` (% of its own height) while its nearest `[data-anim-parallax-trigger]` ancestor, or itself, crosses the screen. `.parallax-layer(@travel)` in `_mixins.less` sets both and overhangs the frame by `@travel`% above and below, for a photo drifting inside a clipped frame. `data-anim-start="top top"` times it from the top of the page (an inner page's hero; the home hero's photo holds still); `data-anim-disable-mobile` holds it still below lg. An ancestor with `overflow: hidden` between the trigger and the page is a scroll container and the timeline runs on it instead of the page (the photo then stands still and jumps when that box changes height): clip such a box with `overflow: clip`. Keep it to one layer per section, or one per card in a short row (the Where-next cards, a story's photos); never on every tile of a long slider.

```html
<div class="pillar__frame" data-anim-parallax-trigger>
  <div class="pillar__layer" data-anim="parallax"><img …></div>
</div>
```

## Tucked sheet — `data-anim="tuck"`
`--tuck` runs 0 to 1 on the element as the page scrolls from `data-anim-wait` px over `data-anim-run` px (design px; 40 and 120), for the CSS to move it by, and on the way back up it holds until the shorter run home, `data-anim-back` px from the wait (50), catches it. `--tuck` eases towards that target on the ticker (`TUCK_EASE`, 90% in about 0.6s), so the sheet glides however fast the page is flicked. The announcement bar's sheet: `transform: translateY(calc((var(--tuck, 1) - 1) * 100%))`, its corners rounding with it. Unset without the script, so the sheet sits in place.

## Scroll progress — `data-anim="progress"`
`--anim-progress` runs 0 → 1 on the element as it passes (`data-anim-start` / `-end`), for CSS to hang a scroll-linked state on.

## Stack — `data-anim="stack"`
gsa.uk.com's panels, for the home page's three pillars (`pillar.stack`). On adjacent sections that each carry it, from lg:
- Each section holds still with its content (its first child's box) centred on the screen, never lower than its top on the top of the screen; content taller than the screen holds with its foot 24px above the screen's foot (`STACK_FOOT`), its top seen on the way in, so all three pillars hold alike with room above the title and below the buttons (54 above the top at 1920 × 945). A parallax inside a stacked section (`data-anim="parallax"`, the pillar photo) is driven by the stack, timed by the scroll alone across the whole time its section is on screen, from its first showing until the next one holds over it (`--stack-drift-from` / `--stack-drift-to`, `is-stacked`): its own view timeline jumped under the hold and the wipe. `is-held` makes it `position: sticky` at `--stack-top`, from its top reaching the top of the screen until the next one covers it, so the switch is never seen.
- The next section is drawn up under the held one's foot below the screen (`--stack-pull`, a negative margin), so its edge starts rising from the foot of the screen the moment the held one stops, with no dead scroll between. It is uncovered over it across one screen of scroll, its top corners rounded (`is-incoming`, `--radius-lg`), and docks exactly where it then holds: its top edge rises while its content stands where it will rest, and nothing moves on when it docks. The section's first child is clipped at the section's own top edge (`is-incoming`, `--stack-pad`), and that child's children move against the scroll (`stack-uncover`, `--stack-shift`), so they stand still.
- Under a mouse or trackpad, where Lenis moves the page from script, `anim.js` sets both moves in the same frame as Lenis's scroll (`is-scripted`): a CSS scroll timeline ran a frame behind Lenis, and the content that should stand still shook by up to 20px, worst as the scroll turned back up. Elsewhere (touch, native scrolling) they run on a CSS scroll timeline (`scroll(root)` with `animation-range: var(--stack-from) var(--stack-to)` in px, `06-animation/_stack.less`); with neither, the sections simply scroll. A held section lets go once the next one holds.
- `anim.js` measures the run in its natural layout on every ScrollTrigger refresh (the classes come off for `refreshInit` and go back on at `refresh`), so every other trigger on the page measures true positions too.
- An incoming section is uncovered as it stands: its reveals are dropped (shown at once), and elements marked `data-anim-stack-dim` (the pillar's lead, copy, bullets, proof points and buttons) sit at 35% until 40% of the way, then come up to full as it docks (`stack-dim`). Its split title rises line by line once the rising edge has uncovered half of it; its counters are dropped too and show their figures as they stand (they count only in the first section).
- A keyboard user tabbing into a held section the next one has partly covered is scrolled back to see it whole.
- Below lg, under reduced motion, or for a section with no stacked neighbour, nothing changes: the sections scroll and reveal as usual.

## The page entrance
Hero-tier steps play on load as one GSAP timeline (`Anim.entrance`), in `data-anim-order`: each order starts 0.4s before the one before it has landed (`data-anim-lead` on a step sets another lead; negative for a pause), and a step lands when its animation ends or `data-anim-landed` seconds in. Steps the timeline cannot own (the title's lines and the glides, on the compositor) are started at once with their position as a delay on the compositor's own clock, and hold a slot of their length in the timeline: a first load stalls the main thread (the hero photo decoding, fonts arriving), the ticker leaps over a stall, and a step started by a callback in it started late while the next kept its time, so the intro arrived under a title still rising; on a reload, with everything cached, it never showed. On the home page: the photo is simply there (no settle), the title rises line by line exactly as every section title does, 0.11s apart (1); as its last line lands (`data-anim-lead="0"`) the intro lifts in under its fade (2, `fade-up-soft`) and the glass button follows it 0.2s later (2, `data-anim-delay="0.2"`); then the header drops in item by item from left to right, 0.07s apart, on the compositor, each landing under its fade (4: the logo, each link, then the pill fading in place as its button and burger drop in); last, the video card springs in from the right over 1.3s (5, lead 1.2, so it comes as the header is still dropping; `data-anim-duration="1.3"`). An inner page's hero photo is simply there too (its zoom-in was dropped: the photo snapped as the zoom handed it back to the page); its title rises (2, counted as landed 0.8s in, so what follows does not wait out its slow rise), then the breadcrumbs (counted as landed 0.6s in) and the header come together (4). It waits for the webfonts (0.9s at most) so the title is split in its real face, and for the tab to be seen: opened in a background tab, the clock stands still and the timeline would leap on the first look, its steps firing together (the description with the title); hidden mid-way, the timeline's own steps pause while the compositor's run on. The setup and its measuring are done before the entrance's clock starts, and the work the page's `load` event sets off (ScrollTrigger's refresh) waits until the entrance has played, so no frame of it stalls. Anything else on the first screen follows the entrance instead of racing it: it waits until the entrance's steps have landed (by their `data-anim-landed`, so the inner hero photo's 2.6s settle holds nothing up), and a scroll before then releases it at once. An entrance held back by the page change has no landing time until it starts, so they first wait for it to start (`entranceWaiting`). A reveal already on the first screen at load takes its start unclamped (a clamped one sat at 0, which a page at rest never passes, so it waited for a scroll).

## Between pages
A link to another of the site's pages uncovers the next page over this one the way the pillars stack: the old page holds still and the next one's top edge rises from the foot of the screen with its corners rounded (`--radius-lg`), its content standing where it will rest, squaring off as it docks (0.9s, `--ease-in-out`). It is the browser's own cross-document view transition (`@view-transition` in `06-animation/_page-transition.less`, under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`); browsers without it change page as before, Back and Forward play it too. While it plays the browser hit-tests only the root: the head script (on `pagereveal`) lets a press end it and sends the click to what was under the pointer, `header.js` waits for it to end before reading the ground under the pill, and the entrance waits 0.45s into it, once the edge has passed the hero title (`window.pageRevealed`, read by `Anim.init`). The head script marks a page reached from another of the site's pages (a same-origin referrer, not a reload) with `anim-carried` on `<html>`, and `data-anim-carry` (the header's bar) then shows at once instead of joining the entrance, so the header does not drop in again on every page. A first visit and a reload play the header's drop-in.

## Sliders
- **Card rows** (`slider.js`) — built once the row comes within 1.5 screens (Swiper re-measures a row on every photo that loads inside it, which stalled the page entrance when every row was built at load). `data-slider` on the `.swiper`, `data-slider-gap="20"` (design px). Drags freely with momentum and eases to rest on the house curve; `data-slider-mode="snap"` rests on a card; `data-slider-rewind` makes the arrows wrap round so neither is ever dimmed. Arrows: `[data-slider-prev]` / `[data-slider-next]` inside the nearest `[data-slider-scope]`; in the same scope `[data-slider-current]` / `[data-slider-total]` show the count ("01 / 03") and each `[data-slider-tab]` goes to its card, carrying `aria-current` while that card shows. Lazy photos in the row are loaded a screen early. On every change of card the row dispatches `slider:change` (`detail.index`), which `stories.js` listens for. `data-slider-centred` keeps the active card centred and `data-slider-loop` runs the row round (the count follows the real card); a `[data-slider-caption]` in the scope shows the active card's `data-caption` (the gallery).
- **Full-width slides** (`stage.js`) — `data-stage` on the block, `data-stage-slide` on each slide (the first with `is-active`), `data-stage-media` on the photo wrapper, `data-stage-title` on the title, `data-stage-item` on whatever else should follow it, `data-stage-prev` / `-next` on the arrows, `data-stage-current` / `-total` for the count, `data-stage-progress` on the fill of a progress line and `data-stage-nav` on the whole foot. The next slide wipes in over the current one (1.4s, `glide`) while both photos drift 16% and the new one settles from 1.12; the old copy fades, the new title rises line by line and the items follow; the count rolls. `data-stage-autoplay="4500"` fills the progress line over the delay and pauses while the pointer is on a slide's card, on keyboard focus, off screen and in a background tab; with no autoplay the line shows the position. One slide per swipe or drag (`a, button` ignored), arrow keys while the stage has focus, instant swaps and no autoplay under reduced motion. `data-stage-tone` on the block mirrors the active slide's own `data-stage-tone`, for CSS to recolour the arrows. The first slide's copy and the foot rise in once as the stage scrolls into view (`top 72%`), after the webfonts.
- **Marquees** (`marquee.js`) — built once the lane comes within a screen; `data-marquee="left|right"` on a track whose first child is the one list (give it a trailing gap equal to the item gap so the loop is seamless); the lane is the track's parent and must clip. The list is cloned to fill the lane (copies `aria-hidden`, their links out of the tab order) and the track runs as one compositor animation at `data-marquee-speed` design px a second (40), only while the lane is near the screen; a rebuild (fonts, load, resize) carries on from the same point. Optional: `[data-marquee-prev]` / `[data-marquee-next]` in the nearest `[data-marquee-scope]` step one item (a second click mid-step adds to the first), `data-marquee-pause` eases it to a stop under a mouse, `data-marquee-drag` lets it be dragged and flicked (vertical scrolling kept; a drag never follows the link it started on). Reduced motion: no copies, no animation; the lane gets `is-still` and scrolls sideways by hand, the arrows scroll it by one item.

## Other scripts
- **Accordion** (`accordion.js`) — `data-accordion` on the list, `data-accordion-item` on each row with a `<button aria-expanded>` inside, `data-accordion-panel` on the collapsing element with exactly one child. One row open at a time; the panel moves by height in whole pixels (0.7s, `glide`) while its child fades and lifts in; `is-open` on the item and `aria-expanded` on the button are the only state. CSS keeps a shut panel at `height: 0; overflow: hidden; visibility: hidden`. `data-accordion-closed-mobile` on the list shuts every row below lg at load.
- **News** (`news.js`) — `data-news` on the block, `data-news-item` on each row, `data-news-photo` on each stacked frame in the same order; pointing at or focusing a row lights it and wipes its photo in over the last one the way the pointer moved (1.1s, `glide`; the new photo settles from 1.15, the one under it eases back to 1.06). The clip is written from numbers: Chrome shortens a reported `inset()`, and a tween read from it paired its values wrongly. Touch leaves the rows as plain links.
- **Menu** (`menu.js`) — opened from any `[data-menu-open]`, it grows out of the opener's `[data-menu-origin]` (the header's pill; the button itself without one), as gsa.uk.com's does. The panel is clipped to that box (`inset()` of five custom properties, `--clip-t/r/b/l/round`, set in `_menu.less`, so the width and the height move as separate properties that can overlap without fighting) and fades in over 0.3s. It grows left to its full width at the pill's height (1.1s), and as that settles (from 0.8s) grows down to its full height as its corners go from the pill's round to its own (1.3s), everything on the `fold` curve (`0.5, 0, 0.1, 1`: an unhurried start and a long, soft landing). Its list items (`.menu__item[data-menu-row]`) are masks while they move: each one's contents drop in from above its top edge in turn (1s on `soft`, 0.07s apart, from 1.2s), and the masks come off once they have landed; the other rows (`.menu__top`, `.menu__foot`, also `data-menu-row`) fade in: the search with the first item, and the foot's buttons and links one by one (0.06s apart) once the last item has dropped in (0.3s after its start). Closing lifts the items back out the same way (0.6s, 0.04s apart) and fades the search and the whole foot with them, then from 0.4s folds the panel back up to the pill's height (1s), then from 1.3s narrows it into the pill (1.1s, the same curve, so it settles rather than snaps shut) and fades it at the end (2.45s in all). An opener with no box to grow from slides the panel in from the right. Parent items open in place by height. Focus trap, Escape, backdrop, `inert` on the rest of the page and `lockScroll()` while open; under reduced motion it is instant.
- **Video popup** (`ui.js`, `partials/video-popup.njk`) — `a[data-video-popup][href]` opens its link in a modal `<dialog>` instead of leaving the page: a video file plays in a `<video>` with controls, a YouTube or Vimeo link in their player (YouTube's no-cookie domain). The screen fades in (0.5s) and the frame lifts 24 as it fades (0.9s, `soft`, its tail cut). The Close button, Escape or a click outside the frame closes it (0.4s, the frame sinking 12 as it fades) and empties the frame, so the video stops; focus goes back to the link. Scroll is locked while open.
- **Header** (`header.js`) — the header scrolls away with the page; only its pill (Book a tour and the burger, `.site-header__tools`) stays, fixed where the bar's row put it. The script measures the pill's width into `--header-tools`, the room the bar keeps for it at its right, so the links do not shift. On every scroll (once a frame) it also looks at what lies under the burger, past the header: a photo or video counts as dark, otherwise the first background colour at least half opaque decides, light above a relative luminance of 0.6. Over a light ground the pill gets `is-light`: from lg the white burger turns canopy and the clear glass takes a faint dark tint (`--color-black-5`), below lg the white button takes a hairline ring, so neither disappears on the white and beach sections.
- **Stories** (`stories.js`) — `data-story` on a card, `[data-story-toggle]` on its button (`data-story-close` is the open label, "Close"), `[data-story-copy]` on the copy and `[data-story-more]` on whatever only the open card shows. The copy fades out (0.25s), the card eases to its new height in whole pixels (0.8s, `glide`), the copy rises back in (16px, cut by `landing()`) and the extra parts fade in; closing fades them first, and scrolls the card's top back under the header when it was above the screen. `is-open` and `aria-expanded` are the state. Instant under reduced motion.
- **Moments** (`moments.js`) — `data-moments` on the block, `data-moments-stage` on the stack of moments, `data-moments-item` on each moment, `data-moments-copy` on its copy and `data-moments-photo` on its photo. From lg, with motion allowed, the block gets `is-pinned` and `--moments-count`: it is one screen plus 110% of a screen per further moment tall, its stage sticky, and the moments stacked in one place. Each moment after the first rolls over the last from the right, the pillars' stack turned sideways: across 110% of a screen of scroll (scrubbed, `ease: none`) the panel comes in from the right edge with its left corners rounded (`--radius-lg`) while its content moves against it by the same amount, so the content stands where it will rest, the edge uncovers it and nothing moves on when it docks. The moment under it holds still. Both moves are on held layers (`force3D`, `will-change: transform`); the row used to slide across as one, and its text shivered however its layer was drawn. Each photo drifts 6% of its width per moment of scroll across its frame, the whole time its moment is on screen, until the next has rolled over it. An incoming moment is uncovered as it stands: its photo's wipe is dropped (shown at once) and its photos load as the block nears (a screen below), since standing off to the side they are out of the lazy loader's sight. Its copy (label, title, text, note) is left out of `anim.js` and fades in 40 from the right on the compositor, 0.09s apart, over 0.7s on `rise`, once the edge has uncovered half of it; it plays once, and the copy's items sit on layers the stylesheet holds, so they land with no change. The first moment's copy reveals as the block comes into view, as any section's. Below lg and under reduced motion nothing pins and the moments stack.
- **Tiles** (`cards.js`) — on a screen with no hover, a tap on a `[data-tile]` inside `[data-tiles]` opens it and shuts the others.

## Line masks
A split title's lines sit in `.anim-line-mask` wrappers that SplitText gives `overflow: clip` inline. `_animation.less` replaces that with a `clip-path` that reaches 0.14em above, 0.12em to the sides and 0.25em below each line, so neither italic overhangs nor descenders are cut while a line rises and the layout of the title does not change by a pixel. Each line starts 150% of its height down (`LINES.from`), clear of that lower edge, and surfaces from it. The foot used to be tight to the line's box, which cut the descenders of a title set at line-height 1 until the line landed, and they then snapped in (2026-11-23).

## Script API
`Anim.init()` runs once from `main.js`. `Anim.add(container, { immediate })` animates content inserted later (load more, a tab pane). `Anim.refresh()` after a layout change, `Anim.stop()` / `Anim.start()` around a scroll lock (`lockScroll()` in `ui.js` calls them). `Anim.lenis` is the Lenis instance, `Anim.entrance` the page entrance's timeline once it has started.

## Without script
`head.njk` adds `anim-ready` to `<html>` before first paint so nothing flashes, and takes it off again if the script has not started 0.8s after load, so content is never left hidden.
